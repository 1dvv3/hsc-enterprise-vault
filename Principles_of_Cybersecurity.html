1. The Core Vocabulary

These four terms are constantly confused and are the foundation of everything else.

Term Definition
Threat An individual or group responsible for carrying out cyberattacks — or the potential source of harm
Vulnerability A weakness in a system or process that can be taken advantage of by an attacker
Risk The potential for harm when a threat exploits a vulnerability
Exploit The act of illegally accessing systems, networks or data

The relationship: a threat exploits a vulnerability, creating risk. No vulnerability, no risk — even with a threat present.

2. Social Networking Vulnerabilities

Vulnerability Definition Impact on the individual Impact on the enterprise Mitigation
Account access Unauthorised login using stolen or weak credentials Identity theft, leaked personal photos, financial fraud Employee accounts compromised, corporate data exposed, network breach risk Strong unique passwords · two-factor authentication (2FA) · avoid password reuse
Account behaviour Risky or careless posting or sharing that exposes sensitive information Privacy loss, stalking, targeted phishing Leaked internal information, legal action, reputational damage Educate on safe sharing · use privacy settings · implement a clear social media policy
Hosting platform security The protection the platform itself provides — or fails to provide Personal information stolen, increased spam and scam attempts Brand accounts hacked, customer data compromised, loss of trust Choose reputable platforms · monitor breach notifications · limit data shared with platforms
Identity theft Stealing someone's personal information to impersonate them Financial loss, damaged credit, legal complications Fraudulent transactions in the business's name, supply chain compromise Limit publicly available personal data · monitor accounts · verify identity requests
Phishing Deceptive messages or links tricking users into giving up private information Credentials stolen, malware installed Network access gained, ransomware deployed, funds misdirected Staff training · email filtering · verify senders · never click unverified links
Evil twins Fake or impersonated accounts made to look like real people or brands Deceived into sharing information or money Brand impersonation, customer deception, reputational harm Account verification · report impersonation · monitor for fake brand accounts

Smishing is phishing delivered by SMS. Named explicitly in the syllabus.

3. Cybercrime Threats to an Enterprise

Threat How it operates
Hacking Unauthorised access to systems, networks or data, exploiting technical vulnerabilities
Phishing, including smishing Deceptive email or SMS tricking users into revealing credentials or installing malware
Scamming Deception to obtain money or information directly from victims
Bots and botnets Automated programs; a botnet is a network of compromised machines controlled remotely, used for DDoS attacks or spam
Employee action causing a vulnerability Insider threat — careless or malicious behaviour opening a weakness. Often the largest single risk because it bypasses perimeter defences

Common enterprise vulnerabilities: weak passwords · phishing susceptibility · unpatched software · insider threats · misconfigured cloud storage.

4. The Four Breach Attributes

A cybersecurity breach occurs when unauthorised access is gained to data, systems or networks, resulting in loss, damage or exposure of information.

Attribute Definition Examples of compromise Consequences
Confidentiality Protecting sensitive data from unauthorised access, exposure or theft Personal records · financial data · trade secrets and IP Identity theft, fraud, corporate espionage · legal penalties · reputational damage
Integrity Maintaining the accuracy, consistency and reliability of data Malware altering system data · unauthorised database edits · manipulation of financial or health records Incorrect business or government decisions · loss of trust in data systems · sector-wide disruption
Availability Ensuring authorised users can access information and services when needed DDoS attacks · ransomware encryption · system crashes causing downtime Business interruption and lost revenue · inconvenience · disruption of critical services
Privacy Protecting individuals' personal data from unauthorised exposure Leaked medical records · exposed financial records · disclosed personal identifiers Identity theft, scams, fraud · legal penalties under the Privacy Act 1988 · erosion of customer trust

4.1 Worked Classification

These scenarios come straight from the deck. Note that most breaches compromise more than one attribute.

Scenario Attributes compromised
Email leak — employee sends a customer list to the wrong client Confidentiality · Privacy
Altered financial records — insider changes transaction records Integrity
Smart home device listening — voice recordings accessed by employees Confidentiality · Privacy
Ransomware on hospital systems — patient data locked, doctors cannot access records Availability · (Confidentiality if exfiltrated)
Fitness app data sold to advertisers without consent Privacy · Confidentiality
DDoS attack on a bank — website unavailable for hours Availability
Website defacement — homepage changed to display false messages Integrity
Password database exposure — hashed passwords stolen Confidentiality · Privacy

The tell for each: Confidentiality = someone saw it. Integrity = someone changed it. Availability = nobody can reach it. Privacy = it was personal data.

5. Legislation and Frameworks

Framework Coverage
Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) Sets out how organisations must handle personal information. Applies to organisations with annual turnover over $3 million and some small business operators
Australian Privacy Principles (APPs) 13 principles governing collection, use, disclosure, governance, accountability, integrity, correction, and individuals' right of access. Technology neutral, so they adapt to changing technology. A breach is an "interference with the privacy of an individual" and can lead to regulatory action and penalties
Consumer Data Right (CDR) In designated sectors such as banking and energy, mandates specific data handling with consumer consent and accreditation of data recipients
GDPR (EU) The EU framework, built on 7 fundamental principles shaping collection, storage and processing of personal data

Authorities: the OAIC (Office of the Australian Information Commissioner) enforces the Privacy Act. The ACSC (Australian Cyber Security Centre), part of ASD, provides business guidance.

6. Individual Privacy Protection

Strategy What it does
Password management Strong, unique passwords per service; a password manager with encryption
Multi-factor authentication (MFA / 2FA) Requires a second factor, so a stolen password alone is insufficient
Encryption Renders data unreadable without the key, protecting it in transit and at rest
Secure networks Avoid unsecured public Wi-Fi; use a VPN when a public connection is unavoidable
Personal behaviour Limiting what is shared publicly, checking privacy settings, verifying requests before responding

Private vs personal information — personal information identifies you (name, date of birth, address, email, phone). Private information is what you choose not to disclose. The books you read are private but not personal; your date of birth is personal and should be kept private.

7. Confusing Terms — Quick Reference

Term Meaning Don't confuse with
Threat The actor or source of harm Vulnerability — the weakness they exploit
Vulnerability A weakness in a system Risk — the potential harm arising from it
Exploit The act of attacking Vulnerability — the opening that allows it
Confidentiality Data not seen by the unauthorised Privacy — protection of personal data specifically
Integrity Data not altered Availability — data reachable when needed
Phishing Deception by email Smishing — by SMS
Evil twin A fake account impersonating a real person or brand Identity theft — using a real person's details
Bot A single automated program Botnet — a network of compromised machines
Personal information Identifies you Private information — what you choose not to share
DDoS Floods a service to deny availability Ransomware — encrypts data to deny availability and extort
